IDENTITY · ONE BALL, TWO NAMES

BA4L is the team. Bowling Bro’ is the coach who rides along.

Same ball, same forest green and lime, so it never looks like a second app. Bro’ adds one thing BA4L’s mark doesn’t have: the ball on its way into the pins. The hook is the whole product (meant-to vs actually-did), and it becomes the patch on the bowling shirt.

A · THE PATCHrecommended

Chenille-patch geometry, 1960s bowling-shirt lineage (Pete’s Lebowski brief). The BA4L ball hooking into the rack, type set on the curve. Stitched edge, slab wordmark. Works at 24 px as the ball-and-hook alone.

Tradeoff: the slab face is a third font in the system. Keep it to the patch only.

B · THE MARK, IN SYSTEM
BA4L Bro’

Left: today’s BA4L icon. Right: Bro’. Identical ball, identical tile, plus the arc. Side by side on a home screen they read as siblings. In the app, the coach’s avatar is this mark instead of a letter “C”.

Tradeoff: quiet. No retro flavor unless paired with A.

C · THE LOCKUP
A BA4L THING

Horizontal lockup for the top of the review page, the debrief header, and the Friday tape. “A BA4L thing” is the endorsement line: Bro’ belongs to the team brand, the way a house label sits under a product name.

Tradeoff: needs 200 px of width; drop to the mark alone under that.

HOW THEY FIT TOGETHER
BA4L stays the app, the domain, the icon, the team. Standings, scorebook, records, recap. Public-facing name for the league. Bowling Bro’ is the coach inside it: Review, Debrief, Shot replay, My Game, the lane cam. Every coaching surface carries the lockup; nothing else does. Pete’s prototype content (arsenal, notes, sketcher) lands in Bro’ surfaces. His brief is the product spec for that half; BA4L’s design system is the visual spec for all of it. If Bro’ ever leaves the team (Big Al’s buys it, another league wants it) the mark and the coaching pages lift out cleanly; the ball stays, the “A BA4L thing” line goes.
